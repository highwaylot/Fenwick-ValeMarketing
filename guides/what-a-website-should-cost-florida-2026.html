What a small-business website should cost in Florida in 2026

Fenwick and Vale · Cocoa Beach, Florida · October 2026 · Sources are linked where the numbers are.

You can pay $19 a month for a website or $15,000. Both are real prices for real things. The trick is knowing what you get at each level. Here are the 2026 numbers, with the sources, so you can tell a fair quote from a bad one.

Do it yourself: $19 to $40 a month

Squarespace runs $19 to $29 a month when you pay for a year (Squarespace). Wix runs $17.77 to $39.77 a month (Wix). You do the work: write the words, pick the photos, build the pages. The tool is cheap. Your time is the real cost.

This is a fine answer for a lot of businesses. In a 2025 survey of 406 US small businesses, 83% already had a website, and 41% of those sites were on Wix or Squarespace (Clutch). If you like this kind of work and have a slow month, do it.

Someone places your words in a template: $499

WordPress.com will build a five-page site for $499, plus a plan of $108 a year. You send all the content first. It's done in four business days. There are no revisions (WordPress.com).

That's a fair price for what it is. Nobody writes your copy, fixes your Google listing, or picks up the phone afterward. If you know exactly what you want to say, it works.

An experienced web shop: $2,500 to $5,000

The Admin Bar asked 1,233 WordPress professionals what a typical project costs. The most common answer, from 38.2% of them, was $2,500 to $4,999. Another 22.8% said $1,000 to $2,499. Their median hourly rate was $95 (The Admin Bar, 2025). Those shops had been in business 12 years on average. A newer shop should sit below them.

In Brevard, two shops publish prices. Finish Work Studio in Cocoa lists websites at $1,498 to $2,498 and care plans from $148 a month (Finish Work Studio). Viera Web Design starts at $399 plus $25 a month for hosting (Viera Web Design). Most of the other local agencies don't put a price on the page at all. That's not a crime, but it does mean the price depends on the conversation.

A small agency: $5,000 to $15,000

Small agencies start around $5,000 to $15,000 for a small-business site (Levitate). WebFX starts at $8,000, with timelines of three to twelve months (WebFX). US agencies on Clutch bill $100 to $149 an hour (Clutch).

An agency makes sense when you have ten locations or an online store. For a pool company or a dental office with five pages, you'd be paying for a team you don't need.

The monthly bill after launch

Most care plans cost $100 to $150 a month, and it's rare to see one under $75. The basics are backups, updates, security, and someone checking that the site is up (The Admin Bar). The hosting underneath costs the shop $11 to $35 a month (Cloudways; Kinsta). So you're paying for a person, not a server. Ask what the plan includes, and how many minutes of edits come with it.

How long it takes

A five-page site is one to two weeks of work. On the calendar it takes four to eight weeks. The number one delay is the owner's content: photos, hours, the list of services. Getting it together can take longer than the design and build combined (Elementor). Any date you're promised should start the day your content is in.

How you pay

A 50% deposit, with the rest at launch, is the normal deal for a small project (Penly). Be careful with anyone who wants it all up front, and with anyone who won't put the price in writing.

Where Fenwick and Vale sits

$900 for one page. $2,000 for five. $5,500 to $7,500 for the whole shop. $99 a month for the care plan. The prices are on the Websites page. That's above the $499 template and below the experienced-shop band, which is where a new one-person shop belongs. The extra money over $499 buys written copy, your Google listing set up, and a person in Brevard who answers.
