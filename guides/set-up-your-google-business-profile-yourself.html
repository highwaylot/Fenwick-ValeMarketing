Set up your Google Business Profile yourself, in 20 minutes

Fenwick and Vale · Cocoa Beach, Florida · October 2026 · Sources are linked where the numbers are.

Your Google Business Profile is the box that shows up on Google and Google Maps with your hours, phone, photos and reviews. It is free. Nobody needs to charge you to set it up, including me. Here's how to do it yourself, in about 20 minutes plus whatever time Google takes to verify you.

Before you start: 2 minutes

Use a Google account that belongs to the business, not to a designer or an employee. If someone else sets it up on their account, they own it, and you'll be asking them for access later. Have these ready: your business name exactly as it appears on your sign and license, your phone number, your address or the area you serve, your hours, and your website if you have one.

Step 1: Search for your business. 1 minute

Type your business name into Google. If a panel shows up with your name, look for the words "Own this business?" That means nobody has claimed the profile yet (Google Business Profile Help). Click it. If no panel shows up, search for "Google Business Profile," click Manage now, and add your business.

Step 2: Prove it's yours. 5 minutes, then some waiting

Google will ask you to verify that you run the business. Google picks the method and tells you what it needs. Some methods finish the same day. Some take days. Start now, so the waiting happens while you do the rest.

Step 3: Fill in every field. 8 minutes

  • Name. Your real name, exactly as it's on your sign. Don't add your city or words like "best" or "cheap." That breaks Google's rules and can get the profile suspended (Google's profile policies).
  • Primary category. Pick the one that says what you are: pool cleaning service, HVAC contractor, dentist, auto repair shop. Add more categories only for things you actually do.
  • Hours. Real hours, including holidays. A wrong "open" sign costs you the customer who drove over.
  • Phone and website. The number someone answers. The site that loads.
  • Service area. If you go to the customer, list the towns you'll drive to.
  • Services and description. Plain words. What you do, what you don't, and what it costs if you can say.

Step 4: Add real photos. 3 minutes

The front of the shop, the truck, you, your work. Phone photos are fine. Skip stock photos; people can tell. Add a few more every month.

Step 5: Make your review link. 1 minute

In your profile, find "Ask for reviews." Google gives you a short link and a QR code you can share by text, by email or on a receipt (Google). Print the QR code and put it at the counter and on invoices.

Then keep it alive: 5 minutes a week

Ask every customer the same way, with that link. Don't offer a discount or a gift for a review, don't ask only the happy ones, and don't let staff or family post. Google removes those reviews and can block your profile from getting new ones (Google; Google on restrictions). Don't send your whole old customer list at once, either. Getting too many reviews too fast is a suspension risk (Whitespark).

Reply to every review within a week. In a 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults, 89% expected owners to reply, and 81% expected the reply within a week (BrightLocal).

Why this matters

In that same survey, 47% of people said they won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and 68% want a rating of four stars or more (BrightLocal). Star rating, review count and how recent the reviews are all rank among the top 15 factors for showing up in the Maps results, and rating is the number one thing that makes people pick one listing over another (Whitespark). The profile is free. The reviews come from doing the work and asking.

When you don't need me: if your profile now shows the right hours, real photos, and new reviews coming in each month, you don't need Fenwick and Vale for Google. The $350 setup is for people who'd rather pay than spend the 20 minutes.
