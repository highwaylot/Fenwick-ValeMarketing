FleetCleared redesign · round 2 · The Dispatcher

The status board. Round 2.

Same board. Five palettes, one recommended, a live switcher so you can see each one on every screen. One new control: EN | ES and day/night in a single object, top right of every screen. Nine website screens, nine social pieces, and the dash card from the front, the back, and the fuel island.

Part 01 · Concept and voice

The dispatch office, not the agency.

Round 1 stands. Charcoal plate. Band on the top edge. One green square that means cleared. Big stencil numbers. A sentence a dispatcher writes on the whiteboard: "Audit in 41 days. 7 of 10 yes." It won because it looked like a tool. Status, number, date, done or not done. Nothing on it tried to sell anything.

What changed in round 2. Four things. One: colors. The trio was a guess; now it is one of five, each tested on every screen, each with its weakness written down. Two: Spanish is on the board, not under it. The switch sits where the eye already goes, top right, and the Spanish screens are written, not translated. Three: red is gone. A "no" in the check is a hollow square with an orange dot: it is on tonight's list, not a siren. Four: day mode. The board is still the brand surface at night; by day the same components sit on clipboard paper, and the band is the thing that doesn't move.

Borrowed from The Bilingual (06). The segmented pill with the active language filled, now drawn in board tokens. The rule that "Se habla español" is a chip the consultant's own statement earns, never a flag. The Spanish standing lines already in use: "Sin robollamadas, nunca," "La fuente oficial en cada página," "Nada se guarda." And the observation that the first message from Laredo is a WhatsApp, so the Spanish consultant card gets that button. Nothing else: the cream, the equals sign and the paired headline belong to that concept.

Kept from today's brand: green as the "cleared" color; "No robocalls, ever" and "Sources on every page" word for word; the guides and two tools as the whole front door; the non-affiliation line on everything public. Replaced: the shield; the soft off-white as the default ground; the "bad inspection finds you" headline; the tilted screenshot on social.

The voice is a good dispatcher's. Short sentences. The number first. No adjectives. It never says "urgent"; it says "15 days from the date on your notice." It never says "trusted"; it says "their words, not ours." It never says "don't worry"; it says "here's tonight's list."

Wordmark: FLEET in Barlow Condensed 800, CLEARED in Big Shoulders Stencil 900. CLEARED takes the "cleared" color of whatever palette is showing; deep green on paper; plate color on the band.

The color study: five palettes

Each is a full token set: board, panel, raised, line, band and its ink, cleared and its ink, caution and its ink, text, dim text, and the day-mode paper values. Two sentences on each, from the cab, the yard or the road. The "show" button re-themes every mockup on this page. Recommendation: Cones, the round-1 trio. It is the only set where band, cleared and caution are three hues a tired eye separates at arm's length; every other set makes two jobs share a color.

The distinctive-asset set

Five things, unchanged from round 1, used identically on web, social, print and the truck. The controls cluster is not an asset; it is a control, and it is drawn to disappear.

The band

A strip across the top edge of every surface with FLEETCLEARED stenciled in plate color. Page header, card, dash card, decal, envelope. Same place, day or night.

The green square

A rounded square with a check. The mark, the bullet, the "yes" button, the done state. Hollow = not yet. "?" = not sure. Hollow with a dot = a no, on tonight's list.

Charcoal + band + green

Whatever the band's color, the trio is dark plate, one warm action color, one cool "cleared." Agencies and scammers are blue, white and red.

Stencil numbers

The number is the message: 15 days, 16 rules, 10 questions, 0 robocalls. Readable from the next pump over.

The board sentence

"[Thing] in [N] days. [X] of [Y] [state]." In Spanish: "Auditoría en 41 días. 7 de 10 sí." Under it, always: No robocalls, ever / Sin robollamadas, nunca.

The controls cluster

One object, top right of every screen: a two-segment language switch and a two-glyph theme switch in a single bar, 32 px tall on desktop, 36 px on the phone, where it also repeats in the thumb bar at the bottom. The active segment is filled with the band color. It is drawn in the board's own tokens so it reads as part of the tool, not a widget. Shape borrowed from The Bilingual's pill; the fill and the square corners are ours.

  • Rules. Language first, theme second, always in that order. The two halves share one border so they are read as one thing.
  • Switching language changes the words. Nothing else moves: same layout, same numbers, same band.
  • Switching theme changes the ground: board at night, clipboard paper by day. The band, the mark and the square keep their colors.
  • Default follows the device for theme and the browser for language; the choice is remembered on the device. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Not on social cards or printed pieces. Those are one language each, written as such.

Part 05 · Where the eyes go

The dash card is the Domino's car.

The one object a trucker already keeps: a card on the dash with the dates that matter. Ours is a 4×6 charcoal card, band on top, three write-on lines, dry-erase coat, pen loop and magnet strip on the back. Useful enough to stay in the cab a year; visible through the windshield at every fuel island it parks at. Front, back and in situ below.

Why this object, in five lines

  • It gets kept. A sticker is decoration; a card with your own dates on it is a tool.
  • It gets seen. Fuel island, wash, inspection lane: an orange band on charcoal from twenty feet, through glass.
  • It says the promise once, on the back, in two languages, and then shuts up.
  • It costs about 40 cents at 500 on 14 pt stock with a dry-erase coat; about $1.10 with the magnet. Hand them out; don't mail them.
  • The decal version: a 1×6 in. band for the inside windshield corner, stencil only: FLEETCLEARED · NO ROBOCALLS, EVER. Never on the door where the USDOT number lives.

The touchpoint plan, refined

Cost tiers are labeled estimates. One rule everywhere: the band, the number, the standing line, in the reader's language, and nothing that could be read as official where officials stand.

Part 06 · Why it will be remembered

Seen once at pump 7. Recognized at 11 p.m.

A trucker who glanced at the dash card in the next truck at a Love's in Temple will recognize FleetCleared three months later on his phone because every element that reaches his eye is the same element, in the same place, doing the same job. The band is always on the top edge, so it is where his eye lands first on a card, a post and a web page alike, and it is the same band whether the page is dark in the sleeper or paper in the office. The green square is always "done," so when the readiness check turns a row green it is the card's check box again. The big stencil number is always the message, so "15 days" on the crisis page is the same voice as "16" on the Facebook card he scrolled past in July, and "16 fallas" on the card his cousin forwarded on WhatsApp. Charcoal, one warm band, one cool green is a set nobody else in his inbox or his mailbox uses: the agency letters and the scam letters are blue, white and red, and every "compliance service" ad copies them. We look like the cab at night and the cone on the shoulder, which is to say we look like his own working life and not like a government or someone pretending to be one. The sentence shape never changes either: "Audit in 41 days. 7 of 10 yes." He has read it on a board his whole career, in whichever language the dispatcher wrote it. Memory is not built by a clever idea; it is built by a few plain things repeated identically for years until the shape alone says the name. That is why there are five assets and not fifteen, why the palette study changes the color of the band but never its place, and why none of the five is a slogan he would have to recall. He only has to recognize a band on a charcoal plate.

Part 07 · Compliance self-check

Each hard rule, and where it holds.

  • Nothing governmental. No seal, eagle, star, stripe, badge outline or blue-and-white anywhere, in any of the five palettes. The shield is retired; the mark is a checklist row with no containing shape. No "NOTICE," "OFFICIAL," or letterhead styling. The mail concept forbids window envelopes, stamps, seals and dollar figures. Nothing is ever placed at a weigh station or inspection site. Sign Green is flagged in its own rationale as the one palette that edges toward the road department, which is a reason it is not the default.
  • Stencil is not uniform. Stencil type appears only in the wordmark, the big numbers and the band on physical pieces; never in body text, never with camouflage, insignia or rank-like layouts. If the founder test in Part 8 hears "military" or "law enforcement," CLEARED moves to plain condensed and nothing else changes.
  • No "verified / vetted / trusted / certified / approved / endorsed." Consultant card and directory use "their statement," "their words," "según ellos," and "FleetCleared does not review or rate consultants." No stars, no ratings, no ranking; the directory states its order is distance. The green square on a directory row means "selected to compare," and the tray says so.
  • No deadline threats or penalty figures. The only day counts shown (15, 60, 45, 41) are the regulation's, cited to 49 CFR 385.321, counted from the reader's own notice date, or the reader's own typed audit date (concept). No dollar figures. No "action required," "past due," "urgent," "final." Red is removed from the system entirely; a "no" is a hollow square with a dot. The fake-letter card describes the scam cues without reproducing them.
  • Non-affiliation line on everything public. Homepage (desktop, both phones), crisis landing, consultant card, directory, guide page, question 6, result screen, all six social cards, the story, the cover, both sides of the dash card and the mail concept each carry "Not affiliated with the FMCSA or the U.S. Department of Transportation" or its Spanish, abbreviated to "U.S. DOT" / "USDOT" only in social footers and the ticker where space forces it.
  • No business-model words carrier-side. Homepage, crisis page, consultant card, directory and result screen never say lead, pricing or tier. The recruiting card (consultant-facing) says "free to list" and "nothing is shared or resold."
  • No stock photos, no mascots. All artwork is inline SVG of objects: a card, a board row, a windshield. No cartoon truck, no trucker character.
  • Never implies danger. Copy says "missing paperwork, not missing safety," "you have time, and a list," "all three are paperwork." No crash imagery, no red anything.
  • No flags. Spanish is marked by words ("Se habla español," the ES segment), never by a flag, on every surface including the chip and the Spanish card.
  • Every mock claim is true today or tagged concept. True: 7 guides, 10-question check, the 16 automatic-fail rules and their citations, MCS-150 tool, tools run on-device, directory opening 2027, first wave of about 15, hand-screened signups, free biennial update. Tagged concept: the live directory, its list view and sample listings, the "pick one" buttons, the on-phone date counter and the "audit in 41 days" line, and all Spanish site copy (the guides are English today).
  • Controls cluster. One object, EN | ES then day/night, 32 px desktop and 36 px phone (limits: 36 and 40), top right of every website screen and repeated in the phone thumb bar. Drawn in board tokens.

Part 08 · A note to the founder

Test the colors on the card, not on the screen.

Print the dash card in two palettes: Cones and whichever of the other four you keep looking at (my guess is Lot Light). Fifty of each, local shop, about $80 total, 14 pt with a dry-erase coat, no magnets yet. Take both stacks to a fuel island in Temple or Waco on a weekday morning. Hand a driver one of each and ask two things: "What do you think this is for?" and "Which one would you keep?" Count. If anyone says "government" or "DOT" about either card, that color is out. If one card wins seven of ten, that is your palette; the website follows the card, not the other way round. Five drivers is enough to kill a bad option; ten is enough to pick between two good ones.

Same week, two Facebook pairs. First, the "16 violations" card above against the same fact in today's green-shield style, same group, same hour, same weekday, no boost; count saves and shares, not likes. Second, in a Laredo or RGV Spanish group you are already in: the Spanish card as drawn, posted by itself, with no English post beside it. If it gets forwarded, you will see it come back to you on WhatsApp within a week. That is the only Spanish-market test you need before writing a single Spanish guide.

Then the language switch. Before building it, put the EN | ES cluster on the current site's header as a dead button that logs taps (concept; nothing is sent but the count). If it gets tapped, you know the order to write the Spanish pages in: the audit check first, then the 16 fails, then the crisis page. If it does not get tapped in a month, leave the Spanish for the cards and the classes, and don't spend the build. Everything else here waits for those three numbers.

What to leave alone: the guide copy, the sources, the two tools and the words "No robocalls, ever." They're the product. This concept is the box it ships in, and now the box comes in five colors, one of which is the one you already had.
